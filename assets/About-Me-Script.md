# About Me — exact narration script

Read only the quoted paragraphs. Advance the slide after each paragraph. Aim for a natural pace of 125–140 words per minute (about 4–5 minutes total). Pause briefly between slides.

## Slide 1 — Engineering for the real world

Hi, I’m Mohammed Umair Jibran, a Computer Engineering student at York University. I’m interested in the point where hardware, software and people meet. This portfolio brings together my technical projects and my experience at U+ Education, and shows how my approach to engineering has developed through practical work.

## Slide 2 — Why Computer Engineering

What I love most about engineering is the ability to design and create, while understanding how things actually work. Computer Engineering gives me the freedom to explore several layers of a system. I can start with an electrical signal, write firmware that responds to it, and build software that makes the result useful. I enjoy moving between those layers because it helps me see the whole problem. Building something is exciting, but understanding why it works, and why it sometimes fails, is what keeps me interested.

## Slide 3 — My most rewarding co-op learning

My most rewarding co-op learning has come from helping technical teams at U+ Education turn broad requests into something people can use. In my Technology Manager role, I work with volunteer leads across web, software and data projects. I help define tasks, review deliverables and resolve blockers. That experience taught me that technical knowledge is only part of delivering a solution. People also need clear priorities, useful feedback and a shared understanding of what done means. Seeing how my decisions affect the team has made me more thoughtful about requirements, release testing and documentation.

## Slide 4 — Skills I have developed

The main development in my skills is that I now think beyond getting my own code to run. I pay more attention to how requirements are recorded, how another person will test a feature, and what information they need when I hand it over. At U+ Education, I have practised technical review, release quality assurance and coordination across contributors. In my independent projects, I have developed hardware and software debugging skills, especially by testing missing messages, invalid inputs and recovery behaviour. Together, those experiences have made my work more deliberate and easier for someone else to understand.

## Slide 5 — Embedded systems in practice

Two projects show my interest in embedded systems. In my CAN-FD test bench, I connected two STM32 controllers using C, C plus plus and FreeRTOS. One sends temperature and RPM data; the other checks the message before controlling a fan. I also built a Python tool to capture traffic and inject delayed or dropped frames. My RF Asset Finder explores a different communication problem using Arduino and a 433 megahertz radio link. I implemented device IDs, integrity checks, acknowledgements and retries. Both projects pushed me to think about what a system should do when communication stops being reliable.

## Slide 6 — Software that supports a decision

My software projects apply the same thinking. In SentriCode, I built a workbench that brings security findings into a common review flow, with redacted evidence and visible scan status. A skipped check should not look like a clean result. In the packing rule engine, I joined order and product data, validated inputs, and generated deterministic packing instructions. I tested the output with staff and documented the workflow. These projects taught me to judge an output by the decision it helps someone make, as well as by whether the code runs.

## Slide 7 — Where I want to go next

My career goal is to work as an embedded systems engineer, focusing on hardware and software integration. I want to design firmware for connected devices, work with sensors and actuators, and validate how systems behave under real constraints. Over time, I would like to lead embedded product development projects while staying close to the design and testing work. My co-op experience has shown me that I enjoy both building a solution and helping a team deliver it. Thank you for exploring my portfolio.
