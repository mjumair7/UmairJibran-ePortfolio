What I love most about engineering is the ability to design and create, and to understand how things work beneath the surface. Computer Engineering lets me connect circuits, firmware and software instead of seeing them as separate subjects. At York, my coursework includes embedded systems, communication networks, digital logic, operating systems and databases. My projects give me a way to put those ideas into practice. I especially enjoy building something I can test, observing what happens when an input changes, and working out why the system behaves the way it does.

My most rewarding co-op learning has come from my experience at U+ Education, where I completed an eight-month co-op followed by contract roles. As Technology Manager, I directly managed an eleven-member cross-functional team and supervised more than two hundred volunteers, supporting over three thousand community service hours. ## Slide 4 — A tool adopted by a business

During my Riipen internship with Islamic Books and Souvenirs, from February to May 2025, I built a Node.js packing-rule engine. It joined Shopify-style order data in JSON with product dimensions stored in CSV files. I validated the inputs and applied deterministic rules to generate packing instructions for more than twenty orders. The business adopted the tool, and the work contributed to an approximately fifteen percent combined improvement in order-to-shipment time and packaging costs. This experience showed me the value of a small, focused tool that fits an existing workflow. It also gave me practical experience with data validation and with turning business requirements into predictable software behaviour.

## Slide 5 — Testing embedded systems

My CAN-FD test bench connects two STM32 controllers using C, C plus plus and FreeRTOS. The sensor controller sends temperature and RPM data, while the actuator controller checks message identity, integrity and freshness before controlling a fan. I implemented queues, interrupts, timers and buffers, and built a Python harness to capture traffic and inject dropped or delayed frames. In my RF Asset Finder, I developed Arduino firmware and a tag-to-base-station protocol using device IDs, sequence numbers, CRC checks, acknowledgements and retries. Desktop tests cover corrupt frames, invalid packet types, duplicate sequences and timeout behaviour. These projects developed my hardware and software debugging skills and reinforced the importance of checking failure and recovery paths.

## Slide 6 — Security and payment workflows

In SentriCode, I built a local-first security workbench using Python, FastAPI, Next.js, TypeScript and Docker. It accepts directories, ZIP files and public GitHub repositories, and normalizes findings from several types of security checks. I worked on safe archive extraction, secret redaction, policy gates and scan-history comparisons, and verified the release with one hundred and forty-two automated tests. In my Technology Lead and Executive Director role with the Muslim Student Association at York, I developed the front end and server-side Stripe integration for a donation workflow using WisePOS and BBPOS hardware. I validated terminal data flows and trained volunteers. That system supported more than ten thousand dollars in fundraising at live events.
